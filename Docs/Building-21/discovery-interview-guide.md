# Discovery Interview Guide

## Purpose

Discovery time should be used for questions that research cannot answer. Clients are busy and are paying for expert judgment, so the team should arrive with a research memo and use the meeting to uncover pain, desired capability, constraints, and ownership.

## Before the meeting

Prepare a short research memo covering:

- Who the client is and what the organization does.
- Who the organization serves.
- The organizational structure and relevant legal entities.
- Systems, integrations, data owners, and known reporting flows.
- Publicly available context and questions that can be answered without client time.
- A hypothesis about likely high-value process pain, clearly labeled as a hypothesis.

Bring a working note template. Use `**` in notes to flag anything that requires follow-up, confirmation, an artifact, or access.

## Two required questions

Ask both questions in every discovery interview:

> Tell me about a data or information process you do frequently that is painful or time-consuming and matters.

This looks for **pain to automate**: repeated work, waiting, chasing, reconciliation, manual review, or error-prone handoffs.

> Tell me about something you wish you could do with your data that you cannot do today.

This looks for **new capability to unlock**: analysis, visibility, forecasting, reporting, alerts, or self-service that current systems do not provide.

## High-value follow-ups

- What starts the process, and how often does it happen?
- Walk me through the last real example step by step.
- Which system is used at each step?
- What information is entered, copied, downloaded, or re-keyed?
- Where does the work wait, fail, or require chasing someone?
- What does a correct result look like?
- What exceptions require judgment or legal accountability?
- Who owns the work and who approves the result?
- What would be safe to automate first?
- What artifact would let us understand this accurately?

## Artifact rule

When a client mentions a checklist, immediately ask to see it. Checklists are high-value artifacts because they expose sequence, ownership, timing, exceptions, and the client's definition of done.

Useful artifacts include:

- Checklists and standard operating procedures.
- Exports and sample reports.
- Chart of accounts and fund lists.
- Example invoices, receipts, and reconciliations.
- Approval rules and exception examples.
- Dashboard screenshots or read-only access.
- A recent completed period and one period with known issues.

## Interview output

Within one business day, record:

1. What was heard, separated from assumptions.
2. Pain points ranked by frequency, effort, risk, and importance.
3. Desired capabilities and the users who need them.
4. Systems, data owners, and access dependencies.
5. Artifacts requested and the person responsible for providing each one.
6. Research questions that should be answered before the next meeting.
7. Questions intentionally deferred to a follow-up session.
8. Proposed smallest useful next step.

## Coaching principle

A discovery interview is not a blank-slate questionnaire. Research first, ask only what cannot be learned elsewhere, and use the meeting to test hypotheses against lived process detail.
